# Live precipitation guidance

Farmer View option 7 now shows a separate live NOAA precipitation forecast alongside observed PRISM rainfall. `scripts/fetch_noaa_precip.py` finds the latest published NBM CONUS cycle in NOAA NOMADS, requests the accepted `blend` dataset’s QPF field clipped to Connecticut, reads the GRIB grid, and samples the representative point of each mapped candidate field and the Yale Farm pilot. Original issue files are timestamped under `outputs/forecasts/precipitation/noaa-live/issued/`; `latest.json` points to the active issue. Raw clipped grids and their hashes are retained under `data/noaa-nbm/`.

The current issue (corrected November 6, 2026) covers November 7–20. Each day with a complete local-day chain of NBM accumulations uses direct NBM quantitative precipitation: hourly amounts for about the first 44 forecast hours, then 6-hour amounts, out to forecast hour about 260 (roughly 10 complete days). A partly covered day falls back to NOAA precipitation probability combined with each location's PRISM wet-day frequency and amount; later days with no NBM amounts use the trailing 42-day PRISM mean, and days 12–14 are blended equally with that mean. Those fallback and blended amounts are experimental, not direct NBM QPF. Nothing has a calibrated uncertainty range, and none of it should be read as a precise farm rain-gauge reading. An incomplete NBM local day is never treated as a complete total.

**Correction, November 6, 2026.** Earlier issues double-counted rain: every 6 forecast hours NOAA's files list a 24-hour total first, and the script added it on top of the hourly amounts covering the same hours. On November 6 this showed 208.6 mm at Yale Farm instead of 66.5 mm, and the average 14-day total across locations fell from 53.6 mm to 13.3 mm once corrected. The same bug left days 2–11 without complete coverage, so they had silently used the probability fallback. Originals are retained; affected issues and the correction are listed in `audit/nbm-qpf-double-count.json`.

The precipitation workflow runs from the daily prospective check. It retains the pre-existing history-only PRISM baseline in its original sealed ledger and saves NOAA guidance in the separate issue archive. A daily run will report NOAA download errors. Candidate boundaries are unconfirmed; NOAA values are point samples on the forecast grid and adjacent fields can share the same grid value.

Primary source: [NOAA National Blend of Models](https://vlab.noaa.gov/web/mdl/nbm) and its [NOMADS filter catalog](https://nomads.ncep.noaa.gov/gribfilter.php?ds=blend).

**Correction, November 11, 2026.** Days 12–14 are meant to be a 50/50 blend of NOAA's days 9–11 and the 42-day PRISM mean. When NOAA also gave a chance of rain for those days, the code averaged in the PRISM mean a second time, giving about 25% NOAA and 75% PRISM. Each day is now blended once. Earlier issues are retained unchanged.

## Sealed scoring (from November 11, 2026)

Each issue now also stores totals on PRISM's day (12:00–12:00 UTC, labelled by its end date), built only from direct NBM rain amounts. `scripts/noaa_precip_daily.py` (night run, after the NBM download) seals those totals once per Connecticut day in its own append-only ledger (`data/noaa-rain-prospective`, `outputs/noaa-rain-prospective`). Only PRISM days that start after the seal are included (about 10 per issue). They are scored against the first captured PRISM value, beside the location's last-14-day PRISM mean, the same rival as the history-only rain forecast. A learning version (`learning_daily.py`, model `noaa-rain`) corrects it from scored comparisons. Each lesson's error is capped at ±5 mm, so one storm cannot dominate. The app's accuracy page shows the result.
